The problem

Dog gear is sized by weight. Your dog is sized by legs.

A 25 lb corgi and a 25 lb beagle get the same boot, even though one has legs half the height of the other. The big brands know it. Their size guides list corgis, dachshunds and bassets as "not ideal."

Shafts are too tall

A standard boot shaft runs 3 to 5 inches. On a dachshund it hits the elbow, so the dog kicks it off or walks stiff.

Bellies drag

Four inches of clearance means every puddle and salt slick. Raincoats stop at the ribs, so the belly gets soaked anyway.

Single straps slip

A short leg tapers fast, so one Velcro strap loosens in minutes and the back boots walk off. The fix is a strap over the spine, not a tighter strap on the leg.

Pitter Fit sizing

Measure the pitters, not the dog.

Two numbers, a paper tape we mail you for free, and a size that fits the first time. Boots and suspenders go by paw width. The raincoat and the suspender strap set go by back length. Leg length is never a size here; short is assumed.

  1. Paw width. Paw on the floor so the toes spread, widest point.
  2. Back length. Base of the neck to the base of the tail.
  3. Wrist height (optional). Floor to the wrist joint, just to confirm the boot top clears it.
